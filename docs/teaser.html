Fine-tuning has no immune system.

Every fine-tune, every skill transfer, every quantized deployment today ships on hope: train it, eyeball two outputs, release. Silent skill damage, catastrophic forgetting, and unverifiable medical answers are the industry default — because no tool blocks a bad training outcome.

Trust-Gated Skill Transfer

A teacher AI passes a skill to a learner as an inspectable packet — admitted only after measured held-out improvement, zero collateral damage, clean provenance, and (for high-risk domains) a named human's sign-off. Every packet individually reversible.

Double-Gated Training

SILT trains skills into weights itself — only gate-approved knowledge becomes training data, and the trained result is independently re-examined before admission. Adapters stay removable. Nobody else checks twice.

gate has zero trainer-conditional branches — provable

Honest scope

What SILT is — and what it is not.

SILT is a research system for trust-gated learning. It does not replace human review in high-risk domains, does not guarantee that every approved skill is perfect, and does not remove the need for safe deployment practices. The numbers below are measured on specific runs with specific models; they are not universal benchmarks or hardware promises.

1. Not a universal safety guarantee

A gate can only refuse what it can measure. SILT reduces unmeasured admissions and records every verdict; it does not eliminate every failure mode.

2. High-risk gates still need humans

Medical, legal, and finance skills emit PENDING_HUMAN and require a named approver who re-runs the full gate. SILT enforces the pause; the human owns the decision.

3. Measured, not benchmarked

Every figure on this page came from a specific recorded run in this repository. Performance depends on model, task, compression level, and hardware.
